Step 1 — Your tax profile

Which entity type are you?

This is one of the most important decisions for a Lumpy. Your entity type determines your tax rate, your submission requirements, and your obligations to SARS.

Most common

Sole Proprietor / Freelancer

You trade under your own name. All income is personal income taxed at personal income tax rates. Submit an ITR12 annually and pay provisional tax via IRP6 twice a year.

18% – 45%

depending on taxable income band

Simplified option

Turnover Tax (under R2.3m)

A simplified regime for businesses with annual turnover under R2,300,000. One tax replaces income tax, provisional tax, and VAT. Significantly less administration.

0% – 3%

of turnover — not taxable income

Company structure

Company (Pty) Ltd

Your company pays Corporate Income Tax at a flat rate. You draw a salary (subject to PAYE) or dividends. Requires separate company tax returns and more complex administration.

27%

flat Corporate Income Tax rate

Step 2 — Your deductions

Claim every rand SARS allows you to subtract.

Most Lumpies pay more tax than they need to simply because they don't track or claim their deductions properly. The general rule: an expense is deductible if it was incurred wholly and exclusively in the production of your income.

🏠

Home Office

Proportional to office floor area: rent/bond interest, rates, electricity, insurance. The room must be used exclusively and regularly for trade purposes.

🚗

Business Travel

Fuel, parking, tolls for client visits and work travel. Requires a SARS-compliant logbook: date, destination, odometer readings, and purpose for every trip.

💻

Equipment & Technology

Laptop, phone (business portion), software subscriptions, and any technology used for work. Keep invoices showing your name or business name.

📋

Professional Fees

Accounting, bookkeeping, legal advice, and tax practitioner fees. Keep tax invoices from each service provider.

🎓

Training & Development

Courses, books, workshops directly related to your work and income-generating activities. Keep proof of payment and description of each course.

🏛️

Retirement Annuity

Contributions to an RA fund — up to 27.5% of income, max R430,000/year. Your RA certificate from your provider (RA5 or similar) is the required record.

Keep receipts, bank statements, and records for at least five years — SARS can audit you for this period. The module calculates your home office deduction and vehicle logbook claim automatically based on your inputs.

Step 4 — Your SARS calendar

Every deadline. Personalised to your situation.

Most Lumpies don't know the provisional tax schedule. Sole proprietors must submit an IRP6 twice a year — 31 August and 28 February — and missing a deadline triggers interest and penalties.

The module also calculates your daily provision rate: the exact amount to transfer to The Vault every day, so provisional tax day is never a surprise.
